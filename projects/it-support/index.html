IT Support - Washington & Lee University

Since 2022, I've worked for the Information Technology Services department at W&L. Within ITS, the Client Services division focuses on providing high-quality, closely personalized tier-two support for University faculty and staff. We also manage an array of campus computer labs, lead training workshops, and collaborate with back-end system administrators to ensure smooth and stable access to all IT systems.

Client Services is divided into small (one or two-person) teams assigned to various client groups by category. My team of two supports The College - "the heart of W&L liberal arts education". It consists of 21 departments, including fine arts, humanities, social sciences, journalism and mass communications, as well as minors such as Museum Studies, Film and Visual Culture, and Poverty Studies. We also provide support to all of Athletics and WLUR, the campus FM radio station.

The core of my day-to-day work in Client Services is ticket resolution. Using the SolarWinds Web Help Desk platform, I work closely with our university's Help Desk to manage tickets through creation/assignment, investigation, resolution, and closure:

  1. Ticket Creation

    Tickets are created one of two ways: traditionally, a user will contact the Help Desk with their issue, and if escalation is necessary, the Help Desk will assign it to me. I like to offer a personal touch, so I make it clear to my clients that they can always contact me directly with questions or issues, if that is their preference. In these cases, I am responsible for creating the ticket myself.

  2. Investigation

    Whether the ticket was routed to me from the Help Desk or created from a direct conversation with a client, the next step is to gather data. What is the nature of the issue? Is there an error code to research? Does the problem persist across multiple devices, different browsers, or alternative user accounts? Answering these questions will point me in the right direction to isolate the source of the issue.

  3. Resolution

    Ticket resolution isn't always as simple as sending bulleted instructions or helping a user clear their cache. Often, I need to coordinate with teammates, outside vendors, or the back-end administrators and architects within ITS. This can extend the timeline a bit, so it's important to communicate with the client to keep them up-to-date when a fix is implemented.

  4. Closure

    Before closing a ticket, it's good to make sure that every step of the process has been properly documented - including the steps I took to solve the issue, as well as any outside parties I coordinated with. Collecting this data in Web Help Desk will help others who may face similar issues in the future.

One main difference between tier-one helpdesk support and my tier-two role in Client Services is that I can offer personalized consultation for all sorts of technical needs: hardware upgrades and replacement, project management, software selection and training, and workflow automation. A hands-on, white glove approach means my clients get to stay focused on their work without poring over technical details that may feel unimportant or distracting.

Working in an academic institution, I support an ever-rotating cast of visiting faculty and coaches. For new employees, onboarding is split into three sections:

  1. Pre-employment

    This is the consultation phase - upon receiving the hiring notice from HR, I reach out to the user to ask about their preferences regarding OS, form factor, monitor configuration, and peripheral needs. It's also a good opportunity to send over some preliminary information from our ITS homepage and answer any questions early on.

  2. Equipment setup

    Once I have the information I need from step 1, I prepare the equipment: image a computer (we use a PXE server), apply system updates, install drivers, and pre-configure any needed applications that don't already come with the image.

  3. Setup meeting

    When the equipment is ready, I reach back out to the user to arrange an in-person meeting to get their station set up. Beyond simply configuring the hardware and arranging peripherals, I also ensure they can sign into all necessary services, install relevant print queues from our PaperCut server, give them a quick tour around Box cloud storage, and make any other adjustments we may have missed during previous steps.

In addition to supporting end users, I am also responsible for managing four separate computer labs across campus, totaling 44 machines. Each lab is unique, with its own set of quirks and challenges - changing from term to term with a total replacement every four years. Each summer, the labs are re-imaged to install new versions of their software released since the previous year.

One of the most important facets of the summer lab upkeep is applying updates to software packages. On a cross-departmental team of three, I help to package software used in all labs - 195 separate applications, split across seven different lab images, four of which are applied to my own labs. The process varies for each application, but generally follows these steps:

  1. Copy and rename the previous year's package folder into a temporary scratch space
  2. Check the vendor's website for the latest version of the software
  3. Replace the .exe or .msi with the new version
  4. Update the .bat file to reflect changes in version number
  5. Check for other details that need adjusting - updated license files, shortcut locations, configuration files, etc
  6. Update the PowerShell detection script with new version number to avoid conflicts in SCCM's task sequence
  7. Move the updated package folder back into the network share to be added to the task sequence

During my first months in this role, I co-founded the new User Experience committee, with the goal of overhauling how Client Services interfaces with end users across campus. We created a map of typical touchpoints with employees:

  1. Onboarding
    • First contact
    • Employee arrives on campus
    • Computer deployment meeting
  2. Hardware replacement
    • Email notification
    • Replacement meeting
  3. Role change
    • Lateral move
    • Promotion
    • Department Head rotation
    • Returning former employee
  4. Offboarding
    • Retirement
    • Resignation

With the touchpoints mapped, we built a spreadsheet outlining:

  1. Relevant parties to contact or loop in
  2. Information to convey to or gather from the employee
  3. Hardware to deploy or collect
  4. Internal records to update

With all this data collected and organized in one place, Client Services now has a standardized playbook to reference for all routine interactions with clients, ensuring that service is consistent for all users across campus.
